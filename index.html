Welcome

This digital collection presents the life, works, and historical context of Allama Sayyid Imdad Hussain Kazmi al‑Mashhadi (1901–1975), a prominent South Asian Shi‘i scholar, Quran exegete, writer, and preacher. The journey of his ancestors is traced — from Safavid Persia to Mughal India and settlement in Punjab in 1654. In the 1900s, as the British Empire was fading, and following in the footsteps of his grandfather's family - who had reconnected with the Awadh-Lucknow devotional culture of North India - Allama Imdad became a prominent figure, forging a new chapter in a rich Indo‑Persian scholarly tradition.

This archive documents his education, scholarly networks, publications, and leadership within a leading Lahore-based Shi'i publishing institution - Idara Ma‘arif‑e‑Islam - whose activities and influence extended to England and East Pakistan. Through manuscripts, printed works, and historical notes, the collection preserves a legacy of Shi‘i scholarship shaped by migration, empire, and cultural exchange over a period of five centuries.

Over half a century after his passing, Allama Imdad's scholarly and literary works, journal publishing, and legacy in the diaspora are brought to light. His known works are listed, with links to a collection of digitised editions of verified surviving titles. This includes his major work - 'Quran-ul-Mubeen: Tafsir al-Muttaqeen' - an Urdu translation and exegesis of the Quran first published in Lahore, 1961.
